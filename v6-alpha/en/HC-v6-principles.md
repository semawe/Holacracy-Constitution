# Declaration of Principles — v6 [candidate 3]

> Candidate version 3 — Holacracy v6 project, Sémawé.

---

In adopting this Constitution, we are not merely signing a set of rules. We are choosing a way of being together in service of a purpose greater than ourselves. The principles that follow state what we commit to: what guides our interpretation of the rules when they are silent, and what remains true as they evolve.

---

## 01. We serve a purpose of our own

We recognize that this organization exists for a purpose distinct from our own. In joining it, we freely choose to put our energy in service of that purpose. This is the meaning of our commitment.

## 02. We treat tensions as signals

We welcome tensions (the gaps between what is and what could be) as valid information in service of the shared purpose. We commit to giving them a channel rather than smothering them. This is how the organization learns and adapts.

## 03. We distribute authority without abolishing it

We believe authority is most effective when it is close to the action. We distribute it, we delimit it, we make it explicit. Each Partner holds full authority within explicit boundaries. No one is responsible for everything; everyone is responsible for something.

## 04. We make clarity a condition of freedom

We know that ambiguity breeds insecurity. A Partner who is unaware of their limits is equally unaware of the extent of their authority. We commit to making the structure explicit, so that everyone knows where they may act without asking permission.

## 05. We distinguish governing from operating

We maintain a clear separation between decisions that change the structure (who does what, with what authority) and decisions that move the daily work forward. This distinction protects each person's clarity and the collective capacity to evolve without paralysis.

## 06. We do not set people against rules

We evolve the structure around people, rather than trying to change people to make the organization work. The rules are the framework we give ourselves so that each of us can show up as we are and contribute fully.

## 07. We change the rules through the rules, not through power

We commit to changing our shared framework only through the processes that the framework itself provides. As long as this framework is in force, no one may set it aside or bypass it by unilateral decision. Those who adopted it retain the power to amend or repeal it: that is a founding act, exercised in writing and in the open, never an act of management. The system belongs to all who practice it.

In that same spirit, decisions that evolve a circle's structure cannot be blocked by personal preferences. An objection is valid only if it points to real harm to the organization's capacity to move forward.

## 08. We write only what we honor, and everything that matters is written

Nothing implicit is binding. What is not written cannot be held against anyone. We commit to making explicit what matters: roles, authorities, expectations, agreements. This discipline applies to the Constitution as much as to each circle's governance records and to the relational agreements between Partners. It is a form of respect toward each person.

## 09. A circle's structure belongs to its members

Only the members of a circle take part in the decisions that change its structure and its roles. This rule is no operational detail: it is the expression of the principle of subsidiarity applied to organizational authority. The authority to change the rules of an area belongs to those who are responsible for it.

## 10. We support autonomy without mistaking autonomy for independence

We believe that truly autonomous people create more value than people waiting for instructions. But autonomy is not independence: each role is autonomous within its boundaries and interdependent with the rest. This system supports, at once, the full capacity for individual initiative and the coherence of the whole.

## 11. We do not freeze the polarities

We recognize that human groups navigate between natural polarities: verticality and horizontality, centralization and distribution, individual and collective. This Constitution does not settle these polarities once and for all. It provides a framework that lets each organization hold them in its own way, without suppressing them.
